# Spanish target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover Spanish-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for Spanish.

1. **Neutral Spanish by default.** Use neutral, widely understood Spanish (avoiding strongly regional slang such as «vos», «vosotros» or local idioms) unless the series guide specifies European or a specific Latin American variety.
2. **Tú and usted.** Follow directional register decisions in the series guide. Without a decision, use «usted» for strangers, elders, superiors, and formal settings, and «tú» for family, close friends, and children. Do not shift between «tú» and «usted» within a relationship unless the source does.
3. **Titles and ranks.** Render Mr./Mrs./Ms./Dr. as «señor» (Sr.), «señora» (Sra.), «señorita» (Srta.), «doctor/a» (Dr./Dra.) according to natural Spanish usage (use the definite article «el/la» when talking about them, omit it in direct address). Translate military and police ranks naturally («capitán», «teniente», «sargento», «inspector»).
4. **Japanese honorifics.** Keep source honorifics (-san, -kun, -chan, -senpai, -sensei) only if present in the source text; never add them.
5. **Natural word order and pronoun dropping.** Omit subject pronouns («yo», «tú», «él/ella», «nosotros») when verb endings make the subject clear; keep them only for emphasis, contrast, or disambiguation. Place adjectives and clauses naturally in Spanish.
6. **Register without invention.** Do not add unmotivated filler («o sea», «bueno», «tío», «güey») or soften real profanity. Match the source's tone and strength.
7. **Gender and number agreement.** Spanish adjectives, articles, and participles mark gender. If a referent's gender is unknown from the line, neighbors, and series guide, rephrase with gender-neutral wording (e.g., «esa persona», «alguien») rather than guessing.
8. **Shortening.** Trim redundant words and pronouns first. Never drop negation («no», «nunca», «tampoco»), questions, or hedges («quizás», «tal vez», «supongo»).
9. **Punctuation.** Always use opening and closing question and exclamation marks («¿...?», «¡...!») and standard Spanish quotes («...» or “...” consistently). Use a leading dash (— or -) only when the source marks two speakers on one event.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit up. | Acuéstate. | Reversed action | Siéntate. |
| I don't think he's coming. | Creo que va a venir. | Lost negation | No creo que venga. |
| Are you two in a spat? | ¿Están peleados, compas? | Invented regional slang | ¿Están peleados? |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | Buena suerte, Sr. Dr. Detective. | Mechanical title | Buena suerte, detective. |

## Reviewer checks for Spanish

- Opening «¿» and «¡» are present on all questions and exclamations.
- Neutral Spanish is maintained without unintended regionalisms («vosotros» / «vos» / local slang).
- «Tú» / «usted» matches the series guide and directional relationships.
- Grammatical gender on adjectives and articles is backed by context, not guessed.
