# Korean target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover Korean-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for Korean.

1. **Speech levels and honorifics.** Strictly follow the series guide's directional speech-level decisions (하십시오체, 해요체, 해체/반말). When unspecified, use 해요체 or 하십시오체 for strangers, elders, and superiors, and 반말 among close friends, family peers, or toward children. Never shift speech levels mid-relationship unless the source does.
2. **Natural Korean word order and pronoun dropping.** Place modifiers before nouns and verbs at the end. Omit obvious pronouns ("나", "너", "그") whenever the subject or listener is clear from context, and use "저/제" instead of "나/내" in polite speech.
3. **Titles and ranks.** Render titles naturally after the surname or name: Mr./Ms. X → "X 씨" or "X 선생님" as appropriate, Dr. X → "X 박사" or "X 의사/선생님", and translate military or police ranks naturally (Captain → "대위" or "선장", Sergeant → "병장/중사", Inspector → "경감").
4. **Japanese honorifics.** Keep Japanese honorifics (-san, -kun, -chan, -senpai, -sensei) only if present in the source text and standard for the series glossary ("텐마 씨" or "선배" when localized, or "텐마 상" if the series keeps them); never add them when absent.
5. **Register without invention.** Match the source's formality. Do not add internet slang, dialect, or sentence-ending nuances ("~거든", "~잖아") not supported by the source. Keep real profanity at its actual strength.
6. **Ambiguity and kinship terms.** Do not guess unknown gender or age gaps. Use "형/오빠/누나/언니" or "동생" only when age and gender are established by context or the series guide; otherwise use neutral phrasing or the character's name.
7. **Shortening for reading speed.** Condense verbose clauses naturally while preserving verb-final negation ("안", "못", "~지 않다"), question endings, modality ("아마", "~ㄹ지도"), and emotional weight.
8. **Punctuation and spacing.** Follow standard Korean spacing (띄어쓰기), use standard punctuation ("...", "?", "!", quotation marks), and use a leading dash only when the source marks two speakers on one subtitle event.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit up. | 누워. | Reversed action | 일어나 앉아. |
| I don't think he's coming. | 그 사람이 올 것 같아요. | Lost negation | 안 올 것 같아요. |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | 행운을 빌어요, 미스터 형사. | Mechanical title | 행운을 빕니다, 형사님. |
| Could you help me? (to a boss) | 좀 도와줄래? | Wrong formality (반말 to superior) | 좀 도와주시겠어요? |

## Reviewer checks for Korean

- Speech levels (하십시오체/해요체/반말) and subject/object honorifics ("~시~", "저/나") match the directional relationship.
- Verb-final negation ("안", "못", "~지 않-") and modality ("~겠-", "~ㄹ 것 같다") are preserved.
- Kinship terms ("형/오빠/누나/언니") and titles are supported by context and not guessed.
- Korean word spacing (띄어쓰기) is clean and no unnecessary pronouns ("당신", "그녀") remain.
