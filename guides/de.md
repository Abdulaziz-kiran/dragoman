# German target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover German-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for German.

1. **Du and Sie.** Follow directional register decisions in the series guide. Without a decision, use «Sie» (capitalized «Sie», «Ihnen», «Ihr») for strangers, elders, superiors, and formal settings, and «du» for family, close friends, and children. Keep asymmetrical address when appropriate and never switch mid-relationship without a source cue.
2. **Titles and ranks.** Render Mr./Mrs./Ms./Dr. as «Herr» / «Frau» + surname, and «Doktor» (Dr.) + surname («Herr Doktor Müller» or «Dr. Müller» as context fits). Translate military and police ranks naturally («Hauptmann» or «Kapitän» at sea, «Leutnant», «Feldwebel» / «Sergeant», «Inspektor»).
3. **Japanese honorifics.** Keep source honorifics (-san, -kun, -chan, -senpai, -sensei) only if present in the source text; never add them.
4. **Natural word order and pronouns.** Follow German V2 main-clause and verb-final subordinate-clause word order; avoid English word order calques. Keep subject pronouns (German does not drop them), using natural spoken contractions («hab'», «'ne») only when the character's voice warrants it.
5. **Register without invention.** Do not add unmotivated modal particles or filler («halt», «eben», «krass», «Alter») absent from the source. Preserve actual profanity and slang at the source's strength.
6. **Gender and number agreement.** German nouns, articles, and pronouns require grammatical gender. If a person's gender is unresolved from the line, neighbors, and series guide, rephrase (e.g., «diese Person», plural, or passive) rather than guessing «er» or «sie».
7. **Shortening.** Condense wordy phrasing first without breaking German verb brackets. Never drop negation («nicht», «kein», «nie»), questions, or hedges («vielleicht», «wohl», «wahrscheinlich»).
8. **Punctuation and spelling.** Follow standard German spelling (including «ß» after long vowels/diphthongs, «ss» after short vowels). Use standard German quotes („...“ or »...« consistently) and a leading dash only when the source marks two speakers on one event.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit up. | Leg dich hin. | Reversed action | Setz dich auf. |
| I don't think he's coming. | Ich glaube, er kommt. | Lost negation | Ich glaube nicht, dass er kommt. |
| Are you two in a spat? | Habt ihr Stress, Alter? | Invented slang | Habt ihr euch gestritten? |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | Viel Glück, Herr Dr. Detektiv. | Mechanical title | Viel Glück, Herr Inspektor. |

## Reviewer checks for German

- «Du» / «Sie» (and capitalized «Sie»/«Ihnen»/«Ihr») matches the series guide and directional relationships.
- German V2 / verb-final clause structure is natural, not an English syntax calque.
- Negation («nicht», «kein»), modal hedges («vielleicht», «wohl»), and tense are preserved.
- Unknown personal genders are not guessed.
