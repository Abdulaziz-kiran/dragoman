# Portuguese target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover Portuguese-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for Portuguese.

1. **Variety.** Use Brazilian Portuguese by default unless the series guide specifies European Portuguese. Keep spelling, vocabulary, and pronoun placement consistent with the chosen variety.
2. **Formality and address.** Follow series register decisions for *você* vs. *o senhor / a senhora* (and *tu* only where regional or series voice calls for it). Keep asymmetrical address when present (e.g., student to teacher).
3. **Titles and ranks.** Translate titles naturally (*Sr.*, *Sra.*, *Dr.*, *Dra.* with surnames; *senhor*, *senhora*, *doutor* in direct address) and render military/police ranks in Portuguese (*Capitão*, *Tenente*, *Sargento*, *Inspetor*). Keep Japanese honorifics (*-san*, *-kun*, *-sensei*) only if already in the source; never add them.
4. **Natural syntax and pronoun dropping.** Omit redundant subject pronouns (*eu*, *nós*) when verb inflection and context are clear, keeping them for emphasis or contrast (*Eu vou, não você*). Avoid literal English word order and unnecessary passive constructions.
5. **Register without invention.** Do not add unprompted Brazilian slang or filler (*cara*, *tipo*, *né*, *mano*) to neutral dialogue. Keep genuine profanity and slang at the source's strength.
6. **Gender and number agreement.** Verify masculine/feminine agreement on articles, adjectives, and participles. If a referent's gender is unresolved from context, rephrase neutrally (*alguém*, *essa pessoa*) instead of guessing.
7. **Shortening.** Trim redundant words to meet reading speed without dropping negation (*não*, *nunca*), question intonation/markers, or uncertainty (*talvez*, *acho que*).
8. **Punctuation.** Attach `?`, `!`, `:`, and `;` directly to the preceding word (no space before them). Use `...` for trailing speech, standard quotation marks `"..."` for quotes, and a leading dash `- ` only when two speakers share one event.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| I don't think he's coming. | Acho que ele vem. | Dropped negation | Acho que ele não vem. |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | Boa sorte, Sr. Dr. Detetive. | Mechanical title | Boa sorte, detetive. |
| Are you two in a spat? | Vocês estão de treta, né? | Invented slang and filler | Vocês brigaram? |
| Maybe you're right. | Você tem razão. | Lost hedge | Talvez você tenha razão. |

## Reviewer checks for Portuguese

- Brazilian vs. European variety is consistent and matches the series guide.
- *Você* / *o senhor* / *a senhora* register matches character relationships without sudden shifts.
- Gender/number agreements and omitted pronouns do not introduce unverified assumptions.
- Negation, hedges, and natural titles are preserved without added slang.
