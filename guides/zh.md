# Chinese target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover Chinese-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for Chinese.

1. **Script variety.** Use Simplified Chinese by default unless the series guide or user instructions specify Traditional Chinese.
2. **Natural Chinese word order and pronoun dropping.** Rebuild sentences in natural Chinese; place time, location, and conditions before the main verb. Drop unnecessary subject pronouns ("我", "你", "他") when context already makes the speaker or addressee clear.
3. **Formal and informal address.** Follow directional register decisions in the series guide. Without a note, use "您" for clear deference toward elders, superiors, or formal clients, and "你" among peers, family, and children.
4. **Titles and ranks.** Render Western titles naturally after the surname or role: Mr. X → "X先生", Mrs./Ms. X → "X女士" (or "X小姐" when context calls for Miss), Dr. X → "X医生" or "X博士" according to profession, and translate military/police ranks naturally (Captain → "上尉" or "船长", Inspector → "督察").
5. **Japanese honorifics.** Do not invent Japanese honorifics. Keep them only when already present in the source text and standard for the audience (for example, rendering -san/-sensei consistently with the series glossary, or using natural Chinese equivalents like "前辈", "老师", "先生" where established).
6. **Register and swearing.** Match the source tone without adding internet slang, meme phrases, or filler particles ("啊", "嘛", "呗") absent from the source. Keep real profanity at its actual strength without softening or exaggerating.
7. **Gender and ambiguity.** Spoken Mandarin "他/她/它" sound identical, so do not guess an unknown third-person gender in writing—rephrase or use a role/name if unresolved. In subtitles, keep "他" only when male or genuinely generic per context.
8. **Shortening for reading speed.** Trim redundant words and filler first while preserving negation ("不", "没"), question particles ("吗", "呢"), modality ("可能", "应该"), and emotional weight.
9. **Punctuation and spacing.** Use full-width Chinese punctuation ("，", "。", "？", "！", "……", "——", "「」" or "“”" consistently), no spaces between Chinese characters, and a single space only when mixing Latin letters or numbers if needed for readability.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit up. | 躺下。 | Reversed action | 坐起来。 |
| I don't think he's coming. | 我觉得他会来。 | Lost negation | 我想他不会来了。 |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | 祝你好运，先生侦探。 | Mechanical title | 祝你好运，侦探先生。 |
| Are you two in a spat? | 你们俩闹别扭啦？ | Added casual filler not in source | 你们俩吵架了吗？ |

## Reviewer checks for Chinese

- Simplified vs. Traditional script matches the project setting with no mixed characters.
- Negation ("不", "没", "别") and hedging ("可能", "也许") were not dropped during shortening.
- Address ("你"/"您") and surname-title order ("X先生", "X医生") match character relationships.
- Full-width punctuation is consistent and no unnatural English-style spaces appear between Chinese words.
