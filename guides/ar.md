# Arabic target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover Arabic-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for Arabic.

1. **Modern Standard Arabic.** Use clear, natural Modern Standard Arabic (الفصحى) by default unless the series guide explicitly requests a specific regional dialect.
2. **Natural word order and pronoun dropping.** Prefer natural Arabic syntax (verbal sentences VSO where idiomatic) instead of English SVO calques. Drop independent subject pronouns ("أنا", "أنت", "هو") when verb conjugation already carries person, number, and gender, keeping them only for emphasis or contrast.
3. **Formality and address.** Arabic distinguishes singular/plural and polite plural or honorific titles ("حضرتك", "سيدي", "سيدتي"). Follow the series guide's directional register; use polite distance for elders, officials, and strangers, and direct singular ("أنتَ/أنتِ") among peers, family, and children.
4. **Titles and ranks.** Render titles naturally in Arabic: Mr. X → "السيد X", Mrs./Ms. X → "السيدة X" (or "الآنسة X" for Miss), Dr. X → "الدكتور X" / "الدكتورة X", and translate military or police ranks naturally (Captain → "النقيب" or "القبطان" at sea, Lieutenant → "الملازم", Inspector → "المفتش").
5. **Japanese honorifics.** Do not invent Japanese honorifics. Keep them only if present in the source text and established in the series glossary (e.g., "تينما-سان"), otherwise use natural Arabic equivalents.
6. **Gender and number agreement.** Arabic requires explicit gender and number (singular, dual, plural) on verbs, adjectives, and pronouns. Never guess an unknown addressee's or third party's gender from thin air; check neighboring lines and the series guide first, and rephrase neutrally (e.g., passive or noun phrase) if still unresolved.
7. **Register without invention.** Match the source's tone without adding colloquial dialect words or filler not present in the source. Keep real profanity at its actual strength without softening or exaggerating.
8. **Shortening for reading speed.** Choose concise Arabic phrasing first, but never drop negation ("لا", "لم", "لن", "ليس"), interrogation ("هل", "أ"), certainty hedges ("ربما", "قد"), or dual/plural distinctions.
9. **Punctuation.** Use Arabic punctuation marks: Arabic comma "،", Arabic question mark "؟", and Arabic semicolon "؛", alongside standard "..." and quotation marks. Keep numbers and directionality clean.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit up. | استلقِ. | Reversed action | اجلس معتدلًا. |
| I don't think he's coming. | أعتقد أنه سيأتي. | Lost negation | لا أظن أنه سيأتي. |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | حظًا موفقًا، السيد المحقق الدكتور. | Mechanical title | حظًا موفقًا، أيها المحقق. |
| Are you two in a spat? | هل أنتما متخاصمان يا صاح؟ | Invented slang not in source | هل بينكما خلاف؟ |

## Reviewer checks for Arabic

- Gender, number (including dual), and masculine/feminine verb agreement match known evidence and are not guessed.
- Negation ("لا", "لم", "لن", "ليس") and question markers ("هل") are intact.
- Arabic punctuation ("،", "؟") is used consistently instead of Latin "," and "?".
- Register stays in clear Modern Standard Arabic without unintended dialectal filler.
