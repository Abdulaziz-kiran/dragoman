# Russian target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover Russian-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for Russian.

1. **Ты and Вы.** Follow directional register decisions in the series guide. Without a decision, use «вы» for strangers, elders, superiors, and official contexts, and «ты» for family, close friends, and children. Keep the relationship asymmetrical when appropriate (student to teacher: «вы»; teacher to student: «ты»).
2. **Titles and ranks.** Render Mr./Mrs./Ms. naturally as «господин» / «госпожа» (or «мистер» / «миссис» / «мисс» only when the setting is explicitly Anglophone and requires it), Dr. as «доктор». Translate military and police ranks naturally («капитан», «лейтенант», «сержант», «инспектор»).
3. **Japanese honorifics.** Keep source honorifics (-сан, -кун, -тян, -сэмпай, -сэнсэй) only if they already appear in the source text; never add them. Decline them or leave them indeclinable consistently with the series guide.
4. **Natural word order and pronouns.** Rebuild sentences with Russian topic–focus word order; do not copy English passive or stiff SVO. Drop subject pronouns («я», «ты», «он/она») when the verb ending or context makes the subject clear, keeping them for contrast or emphasis.
5. **Register without invention.** Use natural, idiomatic Russian without bureaucratic calques or invented slang/filler («блин», «короче», «типа») absent from the source. Keep actual profanity and slang at the source's strength.
6. **Gender and number agreement.** Russian verbs in the past tense and adjectives require grammatical gender. Never guess an unknown speaker's or addressee's gender: check neighboring lines and the series guide first, or rephrase using present/infinitive/plural forms if still unresolved.
7. **Shortening.** Cut redundant words and heavy participial clauses first. Never drop negation («не», «нет», «ни»), question particles («ли»), modal/uncertainty markers («возможно», «наверное», «кажется»), or emotional weight.
8. **Punctuation and typography.** Use Russian quotation marks («ёлочки» or „лапки“ consistently, preferring «...»), the letter «ё» where standard or needed to avoid ambiguity, and an em-dash (—) for dialogue turns on one event when the source marks two speakers.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sit up. | Ложись. | Reversed action | Сядь. |
| I don't think he's coming. | Думаю, он придёт. | Lost negation | Не думаю, что он придёт. |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | Удачи, господин доктор детектив. | Mechanical title | Удачи, детектив. |
| Are you two in a spat? | Вы что, в контрах, ребят? | Invented slang and wrong formality | Вы поссорились? |

## Reviewer checks for Russian

- Directional «ты» / «вы» matches the series guide and stays consistent within each relationship.
- Past-tense verb and adjective genders are supported by evidence, not guessed.
- Negation («не», «нет»), questions, and uncertainty hedges («наверное», «может быть») are intact.
- Word order sounds like natural spoken Russian rather than an English calque.
