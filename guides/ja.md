# Japanese target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover Japanese-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for Japanese.

1. **Politeness and speech level.** Follow the series register decisions strictly for plain (*だ/る*) vs. polite (*です/ます*) vs. honorific/humble (*敬語*) forms. Do not shift politeness mid-conversation unless the speaker's stance or distance changes in the source.
2. **Pronouns and ellipsis.** Drop *私*, *あなた*, *彼*, and *彼女* whenever the subject is clear from context; overuse of *あなた* sounds confrontational or unnatural. Use the listener's name or title instead of a second-person pronoun when natural.
3. **Titles, ranks, and honorifics.** Render titles and ranks in natural Japanese (*先生*, *博士*, *大尉*, *警部*) and place them after the surname where appropriate (*田中博士*). Keep Japanese honorifics (*さん*, *くん*, *ちゃん*, *先輩*) only if present in the source text; never invent or add them.
4. **Natural word order and sentence endings.** Rebuild English clauses into natural Japanese SOV order. Choose sentence-final particles (*よ*, *ね*, *かな*, *ぞ*, *わ*) carefully to match the speaker's established voice without exaggerating gender stereotypes.
5. **Register without invention.** Match the source's tone. Do not add unprompted internet slang, anime clichés, or exaggerated rough speech (*てめえ*, *だぜ*) to neutral lines; keep genuine profanity at the source's strength.
6. **Ambiguity and gender.** Japanese naturally omits subject gender and number; do not force explicit *彼/彼女* or gendered endings unless the source and context clearly establish them.
7. **Shortening.** Condense phrasing for subtitle reading speed using concise wording, never dropping negation (*ない*, *ません*), question force (*か* or rising `?`), or uncertainty (*かも*, *たぶん*).
8. **Punctuation and spacing.** Do not insert spaces between Japanese words; use a full-width space `　` only if needed for readability in place of a comma, or omit punctuation per standard Japanese subtitle style. Use `「...」` for quotes, `…` (three-dot leader) rather than `...`, and full-width `？` / `！` only when needed for clarity.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| I don't think he's coming. | 彼は来ると思います。 | Dropped negation | 来ないと思います。 |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | 幸運を、ミスター探偵。 | Mechanical title | 頑張って、探偵さん。 |
| Are you two in a spat? | 二人ともケンカしてるんだぜ？ | Invented rough ending | ２人ともケンカしてるの？ |
| Maybe you're right. | あなたの言う通りです。 | Lost hedge, stiff pronoun | たぶん君の言う通りだ。 |

## Reviewer checks for Japanese

- Politeness level (*だ/る* vs. *です/ます* vs. *敬語*) matches the series register decisions.
- Unnatural pronouns (*あなた*, *彼/彼女*) are omitted unless needed for emphasis or contrast.
- Negation, uncertainty (*かも*, *たぶん*), and question force are preserved.
- Japanese punctuation, `「...」` quotes, and no ASCII word spaces are consistent.
