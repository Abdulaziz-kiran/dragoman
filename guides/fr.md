# French target-language addendum

These rules follow the general guide and cover French-specific conventions. If they conflict, this addendum wins for French.

1. **Tu / vous.** Follow the series register decisions strictly. When unspecified, use *vous* for strangers, elders, superiors, and formal settings, and *tu* for family, close friends, and children; never switch mid-relationship unless the source shifts distance.
2. **Titles and ranks.** Render Mr./Mrs./Ms./Dr. naturally as *M.*, *Mme*, *Dr* (or *Monsieur*, *Madame*, *Docteur* in direct address without a surname), and translate military/official ranks (*Capitaine*, *Lieutenant*, *Sergent*, *Inspecteur*). Keep Japanese honorifics (*-san*, *-kun*, *-sensei*) only if present in the source text; never add them.
3. **Natural French syntax.** Rebuild English structures into idiomatic French instead of copying passive voice or stacked modifiers. Keep subject pronouns (*je*, *tu*, *il/elle*) as French requires, use natural spoken contractions (*c'est*, *j'ai*), and drop *ne* in *ne... pas* only if the character's spoken register clearly does so; default to standard *ne... pas*.
4. **Register without invention.** Match the source's formality. Do not inject unprompted slang, *verlan*, or filler (*genre*, *du coup*, *en fait*); keep real profanity at its actual strength.
5. **Gender and number agreement.** Check adjectives, past participles, and pronouns against the speaker and referent. When gender or number is genuinely ambiguous, rephrase with gender-neutral wording (*la personne*, *quelqu'un*, passive/impersonal forms) rather than guessing masculine or feminine.
6. **Shortening.** Condense wordy phrasing first (*Qu'est-ce que tu essaies de faire ?* → *Tu fais quoi ?* only if meaning holds), never dropping negation (*ne... pas*, *jamais*), question markers, or hedges (*peut-être*, *sans doute*).
7. **Typography and punctuation.** Use French spacing: a space before `?`, `!`, `:`, and `;`. Use guillemets `« ... »` with inner spaces when quoting, `...` for trailing speech, and a leading dash `- ` only when two speakers share one subtitle.

## Error counterexamples

| Source | Wrong | Why | Better |
|---|---|---|---|
| I don't think he's coming. | Je pense qu'il vient. | Dropped negation | Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne. |
| Good luck, Mr. Detective. | Bonne chance, M. Dr Détective. | Mechanical title | Bonne chance, inspecteur. |
| Are you two in a spat? | Vous êtes en froid, genre ? | Invented filler | Vous vous êtes disputés ? |
| It's a letter from your sister? | C'est une lettre de ma sœur ? | Reversed possession | C'est une lettre de ta sœur ? |

## Reviewer checks for French

- *Tu*/*vous* matches directional register decisions with no unmotivated shifts.
- Gender and number agreements (adjectives, past participles, pronouns) have textual evidence.
- Negation (*ne... pas*, *rien*, *jamais*), hedges, and questions are intact.
- French punctuation spacing before `?`, `!`, `:`, `;` and natural titles are consistent.
